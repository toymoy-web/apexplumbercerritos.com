Who We Are

Local Plumbers, Not A Call Center

Cerritos Apex Plumbing is based in Cerritos, CA, and covers Cerritos plus five surrounding cities: Buena Park, Fullerton, Garden Grove, Los Alamitos, and Lynwood. When you call, you're talking to someone who can tell you honestly what's likely going on and give you a real price before any work starts, not a script reading from a script.

We work on the plumbing issues that come up most in this part of Southern California. That includes drain clogs in older cast-iron and clay lines, water heaters worn down early by hard water, slab leaks caused by shifting clay and adobe soil, and sewer lines that are 40 to 60 years old in a lot of the neighborhoods we serve. Knowing the local housing stock and soil conditions changes how we approach a call, not just what tools we bring.

Most of our calls come from homeowners who have lived in Cerritos or a neighboring city for years and know their home's plumbing well enough to notice when something is off. We aim to match that familiarity from our side: a technician who has seen the same slab-leak pattern in a dozen other 1960s Cerritos homes brings something a generic dispatch service can't.

What We Won't Do

No Guesswork, No Padding The Bill

We won't recommend a full repipe when a repair genuinely solves the problem, and we won't recommend breaking through a slab before an electronic leak trace confirms where the leak actually is. If a job turns out to be smaller than expected once we're on site, the price reflects that. If it's genuinely bigger, we'll explain why before doing any additional work, not after the invoice arrives. We also won't quote a job over the phone without seeing it, since a real diagnosis takes more than a description of symptoms.

Local Knowledge

Why We Pay Attention To Housing Age And Soil

A lot of the homes in our coverage area went up in the 1950s through the 1970s, which means original galvanized or copper supply lines, cast-iron drains, and clay sewer laterals are still common. Add in the clay, sandy loam, and adobe soil under much of the region, which shifts with the seasons. That combination produces a predictable set of failure points: pinhole leaks in old copper, root intrusion in old clay, and slab leaks where pipe runs through a foundation that's moved slightly over sixty-plus years.

None of that is unique to any one street. It's a pattern across the cities we cover, and it's why a technician's first question is often about a home's age rather than jumping straight to a repair.
