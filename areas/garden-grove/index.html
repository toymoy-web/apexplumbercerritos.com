Why Garden Grove Homes Call Us.

Post-1933 Rebuilt Neighborhoods

Sections of Garden Grove rebuilt after the 1933 earthquake have a different plumbing-material mix than areas built later, so we check age block by block rather than assuming the whole neighborhood matches.

Aging Cast-Iron And Clay Lines

Older Garden Grove neighborhoods commonly have clay sewer laterals and cast-iron drains prone to root intrusion and internal scale buildup after decades of use.

West Garden Grove Coverage

Hard Water Mineral Buildup

The same regional hard water that affects Cerritos water heaters affects Garden Grove homes as well, especially older units.

Garden Grove was founded in 1874 as a farming community once known locally as the Big Strawberry. Its history includes two events that reshaped its older neighborhoods. A major flood struck the city in 1916, and an earthquake in 1933 damaged much of the city's older sections.

Homes rebuilt or built after 1933 make up a large share of the city's older housing stock, and that rebuild era is part of why original plumbing materials vary block to block more than in a city built out in one continuous wave. The West Garden Grove area, connected to the rest of the city by a narrow corridor, is served on the same routes as the rest of the city. State Route 22 bisects Garden Grove east to west, and the Christ Cathedral campus near the city center is one of the area's best-known landmarks, useful for describing a location quickly when dispatching a technician.

With five ZIP codes and a population well over 170,000, Garden Grove is one of the larger cities in our coverage area, so we lean on block-level housing age rather than ZIP code alone when scoping a job ahead of time.
