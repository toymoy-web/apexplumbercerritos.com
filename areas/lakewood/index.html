Why We're The Call In Lakewood.

Large, Uniformly-Planned Community

Lakewood was built out almost entirely in the early 1950s as one of the largest planned suburban developments in the country, giving it a highly consistent housing age across the whole city.

Original 1950s Plumbing, Slightly Older Than Cerritos

Lakewood's housing predates most of Cerritos by about a decade, which means original galvanized supply lines here are, on average, further along in their service life.

Lakewood Center Area

The retail corridor around Lakewood Center, one of the largest shopping malls in the country when it was built, brings a steady mix of commercial plumbing calls alongside residential work.

Hard Water Wear

The same regional mineral content shortening water heater life in Cerritos applies to Lakewood homes, with the added factor of several more years of average pipe age.

Lakewood was developed starting in 1950 as one of the largest master-planned suburban communities built in the United States after World War II. It incorporated as a city in 1954 using a new model of contracting out municipal services rather than building a large city government from scratch, an approach that became known nationally as the Lakewood Plan. The entire city was built out in a remarkably short window, which means its housing stock is unusually uniform in age.

That uniform age cuts both ways for plumbing. On one hand, we know almost exactly what to expect walking into a Lakewood home built in the early 1950s. On the other, a large share of the city's original galvanized supply lines are now over seventy years old, a decade or more past what we typically see in Cerritos. That's part of why repipe conversations come up more often here than in some of our newer coverage cities. We treat a Lakewood repipe estimate the same way we would anywhere else: an inspection first, a flat price after, with no assumption about what we'll find just because the neighborhood is older.

Is Lakewood's original plumbing older than what you see in Cerritos?

Generally, yes, by about a decade on average, since most of Lakewood was built in the early 1950s rather than the 1960s. That makes a repipe inspection worth considering even without an active symptom yet.
