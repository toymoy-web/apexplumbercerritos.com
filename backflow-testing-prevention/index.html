In Cerritos.

Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

Backflow prevention devices are commonly required on irrigation systems and certain commercial properties throughout Cerritos and the surrounding cities, and most water providers in this area require annual testing to keep the device certified. Homes and businesses that let testing lapse can face a notice from the water provider, which is often what prompts the call to get it current again. Older Cerritos properties with mature landscaping and established irrigation systems are a common source of these calls, since the devices themselves have often been in place for years without anyone tracking the annual requirement.

Common Signs.

Signs You Need Backflow Service.

A Notice From Your Water Provider

Most testing lapses are caught this way. The provider sends a notice, and it's worth calling before the deadline rather than after it passes.

Visible Leaking At The Device

A backflow preventer leaking or discharging water when it shouldn't often means a failed check valve or relief valve.

New Irrigation System Installation

A newly installed sprinkler system typically needs a backflow device as part of the installation, not as an afterthought.

Buying Or Selling A Property With One Installed

Confirming a device's test status and condition is a reasonable step during a property transaction, since an out-of-compliance device can complicate closing.

Why Annual Testing Matters.

It's Not Just A Formality.

A backflow prevention device stops contaminated water from siphoning back into the clean water supply during a pressure drop. Annual testing confirms the device is actually doing that job, which is why water providers require it rather than treating installation as a one-time fix. A device that passed testing five years ago isn't guaranteed to still be functioning correctly today, since internal seals and check valves wear with age and exposure to outdoor conditions.

FAQs

Common Questions

How often does backflow testing need to be done?

Most water providers in this area require annual testing, though it's worth confirming your specific provider's requirement.

What happens if I miss my testing deadline?

Providers typically send a follow-up notice, and continued non-compliance can eventually affect water service, so it's best addressed promptly.

Do you handle the paperwork with my water provider?

Yes, we submit the required test documentation to your provider as part of the service.

Is backflow prevention installation up to code?

Yes. Installation follows California Plumbing Code standards for device type and placement.

Can a failed test result in a water service interruption?

In some cases, yes, if it goes unresolved for an extended period after a provider's notice. Addressing it promptly avoids that outcome entirely and keeps your account in good standing.
