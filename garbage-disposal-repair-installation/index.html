Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

Garbage disposal calls in Cerritos often overlap with kitchen plumbing that's original to a 1960s or 1970s home, where the supply and drain connections under the sink weren't designed around a modern disposal and dishwasher running off the same line. That's part of why we check the connections around a disposal, not only the unit itself, before calling a repair finished. A kitchen remodel is often the point where these old connections finally get brought up to current code.

Common Signs

Signs Your Disposal Needs Service

Humming But Not Spinning

A humming sound with no spinning usually means a jam or a stuck impeller, not a dead motor.

Leaking Underneath The Sink

Water pooling under the disposal often comes from a worn flange seal or a loose dishwasher connection, not the unit itself.

No Sound At All

Complete silence when you flip the switch can mean a tripped reset button, a dead motor, or an electrical issue.

Frequent Jams

A disposal that jams often, even with normal use, may be undersized for the household or nearing the end of its life.

FAQs

Common Questions

My disposal hums but doesn't spin. Is that fixable?

Usually, yes. That's typically a jam or a stuck impeller, which can often be cleared without replacing the unit.

Can you match the same brand I already have?

We can install most major brands, including a like-for-like replacement if you have a preference.

Will you reconnect my dishwasher after the repair?

Yes. Any dishwasher or water-line connection disturbed during the repair is reconnected and tested before we leave.

How long does a disposal installation take?

A standard swap usually takes under an hour. Adding a new electrical switch or outlet adds some time to the visit.

What size disposal do I actually need?

A standard household usually does fine with 1/3 to 1/2 HP. Larger households or heavier daily use are better served by 3/4 HP or 1 HP, which we can size correctly during the visit.
