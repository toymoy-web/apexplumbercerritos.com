Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

Leak detection is the single largest reason Cerritos homeowners call us, and the clay, sandy loam, and adobe soil under most of the city is a big part of why. That soil expands and contracts with the seasons, which stresses pipe joints under slab foundations over years and is a common contributor to the slab and foundation-line leaks we're asked to trace. Occasional seismic activity in the region adds to that stress over the life of a home's plumbing.

What Usually Brings People To This Page.

If This Sounds Familiar, It's Probably A Hidden Leak.

"My Water Bill Went Up And I Don't Know Why"

A jump in the bill with no obvious cause, like a new leak or a longer shower routine, is one of the most common reasons Cerritos homeowners call us.

"The Meter Keeps Running With Everything Off"

If the water meter dial still moves with every fixture in the house shut off, water is going somewhere it shouldn't.

"I Don't Know Where It's Coming From"

A damp patch, a musty smell, or a warm spot on the floor with no clear source is exactly what leak detection equipment is for.

"There's A Puddle Near The Garage Or Foundation"

Water showing up at the edge of a slab, often near the garage, is a common sign of a leak along the foundation line.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need to tear out flooring to find a leak?

No. Electronic and acoustic detection locates the leak first, so any opening we make is targeted to that exact spot.

What if the leak is under a concrete slab?

Slab leaks are traced with the same equipment and often repaired by rerouting the line instead of breaking through the entire slab.

Can you check a rental property for a tenant's landlord?

Yes. We coordinate directly with property managers and HOAs and can bill or report back as needed.

How long does a typical leak detection visit take?

Most residential leak detection calls take one to two hours, depending on how much line has to be checked and how accessible it is.

Do repairs after a leak is found meet code?

Yes. Any repair following a leak, from a rerouted line to a new fitting, follows California Plumbing Code standards.
