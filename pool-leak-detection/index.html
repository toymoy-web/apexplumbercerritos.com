In Cerritos.

Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

A meaningful share of Cerritos homes, particularly in neighborhoods built out through the 1970s and 1980s, have an in-ground pool with plumbing lines that are original to that construction era. Those underground lines run through the same clay and adobe soil that shifts seasonally across the city, which is a real contributing factor to pipe joints separating or cracking over time, independent of anything happening with the pool shell or equipment itself.

Common Signs.

Signs You May Have A Pool Plumbing Leak.

Losing More Than Normal Evaporation

A pool naturally loses roughly a quarter to half an inch of water per day to evaporation. Losing noticeably more than that, tracked over several days, points to an actual leak rather than normal water loss.

Wet Spots Or Soggy Ground Near Equipment Lines

Standing water or an unusually soft, saturated patch of ground along the path between the pool and the equipment pad often traces back to a leaking underground line.

Air Bubbles In The Return Lines

Air being pulled into the system on the suction side, visible as bubbles at the return jets, can indicate a leak in a suction line drawing in air rather than only water.

Rising Water Bill With No Other Explanation

If interior fixtures check out fine and the pool is the only other major water user on the property, an unexplained bill increase is worth investigating on the pool plumbing side specifically.

Plumbing Leak Vs. Pool Leak.

Why This Distinction Matters.

A leak in the pool shell, liner, or a skimmer housing is a pool-specific repair, generally handled by a pool company rather than a plumber. A leak in the underground supply or return lines connecting the pool to its equipment is a plumbing issue, which is what we diagnose and repair. Figuring out which category a given leak falls into is exactly what pressure testing the plumbing lines tells us, before assuming either way.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you repair the pool itself, or just the plumbing?

We handle the underground plumbing lines feeding the pool equipment. Shell, liner, and pool-specific component repairs are outside our scope, and we'll point you to the right specialist if that's what testing shows.

How is a plumbing leak different from normal evaporation?

Normal evaporation is roughly a quarter to half an inch of water lost per day. Losing water faster than that, especially with no matching drop in humidity or temperature change, suggests an actual leak.

Can you find the leak without draining the pool?

In most cases, yes. Pressure testing the plumbing lines is typically done without draining, though some situations may require partial draining depending on where the suspected leak is.

Is the repair work up to code?

Yes. Any underground line repair we perform follows California Plumbing Code standards for materials and connections.
