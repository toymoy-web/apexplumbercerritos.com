In Cerritos.

Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

A camera inspection matters more in Cerritos than in a newer city because such a large share of the local sewer network, both city-owned and private laterals, is 40 to 60 years old. That age range is exactly where clay pipe joints separate and root intrusion becomes common, and a camera is the only way to confirm what's actually happening inside the line rather than guessing from symptoms at the surface. City reporting on the broader sewer system has flagged similar aging-infrastructure issues at the municipal level, which reflects the same pattern showing up on private laterals throughout the city.

Common Reasons To Request One

When A Camera Inspection Makes Sense

Recurring Clogs With No Clear Cause

If a line clogs repeatedly and cabling or jetting hasn't solved it, a camera shows what's actually causing the backup.

Buying An Older Cerritos Home

Homes from the 1960s and 1970s often have original clay or cast-iron laterals worth inspecting before a purchase closes.

Unexplained Sewer Odors Or Wet Spots In The Yard

A camera can locate a break or separation in the line before it becomes a full backup.

Routine Check On An Older Line

Some homeowners request an inspection simply because their line is decades old and they'd rather know its condition than wait for a failure.

What We Look For.

Root Intrusion, Cracks, And Bellies.

The camera shows root intrusion at pipe joints, cracks or breaks in the line, and bellies where the pipe has sagged and started collecting standing water and debris. Each of these calls for a different fix, from a simple root cutting to a spot repair or a longer trenchless replacement, and the camera is what tells us which one actually applies to your line.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can I get a copy of the inspection video?

Yes, recorded footage is available for your records, real estate transactions, or insurance purposes.

Should I get a camera inspection before buying a Cerritos home?

It's a reasonable request for any home built before the 1980s, given how common original clay and cast-iron laterals still are in this area.

Does a camera inspection damage the pipe?

No, the camera equipment is designed to pass through the line without contacting or damaging the pipe walls.

What happens after the inspection if a problem is found?

We'll walk through what the footage shows and the realistic repair options, from a simple root cutting to a full spot repair.

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Most residential inspections take 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the line's length and how many access points are involved.
