In Cerritos.

Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

A lot of Cerritos bathrooms still have original 1960s and 1970s tub and shower valves, which were built to different standards than modern anti-scald valves required in new installations today. When we replace a shower or tub, bringing the valve up to current standards is part of the job, not an optional upsell, since it directly affects safety during use. Hard water in the area also accelerates wear on original fixtures, which is part of why so many of these replacements happen in homes from that same building era.

Common Signs.

Signs You Need Service Or Replacement.

Water Damage Around The Tub Or Shower

Soft flooring, staining on a ceiling below, or peeling paint nearby often points to a leak at the valve, drain, or surround seal.

Inconsistent Water Temperature

Sudden temperature swings during use, especially when another fixture runs elsewhere in the house, is a sign of an outdated, non-pressure-balancing valve.

Cracked Or Worn Surround

A cracked tub, shower pan, or surround isn't just cosmetic. It can let water reach framing behind the wall over time.

Outdated Fixtures Ready For An Upgrade

Sometimes the plumbing works fine and the reason is simply wanting a walk-in shower or a more modern layout.

Why The Valve Matters As Much As The Fixture.

It's Not Just About Looks.

A new tub or shower surround with an old valve behind it still carries the same scalding risk and temperature inconsistency as before. We check the valve on every installation and recommend an upgrade when the existing one doesn't meet current anti-scald standards, rather than leaving it in place just because the visible fixture is new.

Before We Arrive.

What To Do If A Shower Or Tub Is Actively Leaking.

If you notice active water intrusion behind a wall or through a ceiling below, shut off the water supply to that fixture at its shutoff valve if one is accessible, and avoid using the fixture until it's been inspected. Do not attempt to cut into drywall yourself to investigate; that's something we assess safely once on site.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I have to replace the valve if I'm just replacing the tub?

We recommend it if the existing valve isn't a modern anti-scald type, since the new fixture doesn't fix an underlying safety issue.

Can you convert a tub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, including the plumbing, drain relocation if needed, and valve work involved in the conversion.

How long does a typical installation take?

A direct swap is often a one-day job. A full conversion or remodel-coordinated installation can take longer depending on scope and how much tile or surround work is involved.

Is shower and tub installation up to code?

Yes. Installation follows California Plumbing Code standards for anti-scald valves and drain connections.

What's the difference between a pressure-balancing and thermostatic valve?

A pressure-balancing valve maintains a consistent mix when pressure changes elsewhere in the house. A thermostatic valve holds a precise temperature and is generally the higher-end option of the two.
