In Cerritos.

Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

Sump pump calls in our coverage area tend to cluster around homes with crawlspaces or below-grade areas that collect water during the rainy season. That pattern shows up more in some of the older neighborhoods across our cities than in newer construction with different grading and drainage standards built in from the start.

Common Signs.

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention.

Pump Running Constantly

A pump that never seems to shut off may have a stuck float switch or be undersized for the amount of water it's handling.

Pump Not Turning On At All

A pit filling with water while the pump stays silent points to an electrical issue, a failed motor, or a jammed switch.

Unusual Noises During Operation

Grinding, rattling, or loud vibration during a cycle often signals a failing motor or debris caught in the impeller.

Musty Odor Or Moisture In The Crawlspace

Persistent dampness despite having a pump installed can mean it's undersized, malfunctioning, or the discharge line isn't routing water far enough away from the home.

Why Battery Backup Matters Here.

Power Outages And Heavy Rain Often Overlap.

The times a sump pump is needed most, during a heavy storm, are also the times a power outage is more likely. A battery backup system keeps the primary pump running through an outage, which is the exact scenario a standard pump alone can't cover. It's worth adding to any installation in a crawlspace that's seen water intrusion before.

Before We Arrive.

What To Do If Your Crawlspace Is Actively Flooding.

Avoid entering standing water in a crawlspace near any electrical wiring or outlets, and shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach without stepping into water. Keep pets and children away from the area until a technician has assessed it, and don't attempt to bail out standing water yourself if the source is unclear.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a sump pump if I've never had water in my crawlspace?

Not necessarily, but if the space is prone to moisture or sits at a low point relative to surrounding grading, it's worth an assessment before an issue actually occurs down the line.

How long do sump pumps typically last?

Most pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use, though heavy-use situations can shorten that lifespan considerably.

Is a battery backup really necessary?

It depends on your situation, but a backup is especially worth it if past flooding has coincided with power outages during storms.

Is sump pump installation up to code?

Yes. Installation follows California Plumbing Code standards for discharge routing and backflow prevention.

Can a sump pump be added to a crawlspace with no existing pit?

Yes, we can excavate a proper pit and install a complete system, including the discharge line routed well away from the foundation itself.
