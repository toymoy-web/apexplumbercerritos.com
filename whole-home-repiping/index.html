Benefits.

Why Cerritos Homeowners Choose Us For Whole-Home Repiping.

Stop Chasing Individual Leaks

Once a galvanized or aging copper system starts failing, patching one leak at a time becomes a recurring expense with no real end point. A full repipe replaces the whole system in a single project instead.

PEX Or Copper, Your Choice

PEX is the more affordable, flexible option for most interior runs. Copper costs more but remains a durable, proven choice some homeowners prefer. We'll walk through the real trade-offs for your specific home.

Most Homes Done In 1 To 2 Days

A typical single-family repipe is completed in one to two days, with water restored at the end of each working day rather than leaving a home without water overnight.

Drywall And Access Handled As Part Of The Job

Repiping involves opening walls to reach existing pipe runs. We handle that access work and coordinate drywall patching as part of the project, not as a separate hassle left to you.

In Cerritos.

Why This Comes Up So Often Here.

A large share of Cerritos homes built in the 1960s and 1970s still run on original galvanized steel supply lines, a material that was never designed to last more than about 40 to 50 years and is well past that mark in this area now. Combined with the region's moderate-to-high hard water, internal corrosion inside galvanized pipe tends to progress faster here than in a softer-water region, which is a big part of why whole-home repiping comes up so often in our older neighborhoods specifically.

Common Signs.

Signs Your Home Needs Repiping.

Discolored Water From Multiple Faucets

Brown, yellow, or rust-tinted water from more than one fixture at the same time, especially first thing in the morning, points to corrosion inside galvanized pipe rather than one isolated fitting.

Low Pressure Throughout The House

Pressure that's weak at every fixture, not just one, often means internal corrosion has narrowed the pipe diameter throughout the system rather than a single localized blockage.

Repeated Leaks In Different Locations

A leak repaired last month and a different one this month, in unrelated parts of the house, is a sign the pipe material itself is failing system-wide, not a run of bad luck.

Original Galvanized Or Polybutylene Pipe

Homes built before the 1970s often still have original galvanized steel, and some built through the 1980s used polybutylene, a material now known to degrade and fail from the inside.

PEX Vs. Copper.

Choosing The Right Material For Your Repipe.

PEX

  • Lower material and labor cost overall.
  • Flexible, fewer joints needed.
  • Resists mineral scale buildup well.
  • Not rated for outdoor or UV exposure.

Copper

  • Higher upfront cost.
  • Long, proven track record.
  • Fully rigid, traditional installation.
  • More resistant to physical damage.

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe actually take?

Most single-family homes are completed in one to two working days, with water restored each evening even if drywall or finish work continues.

Will you patch the drywall afterward?

We coordinate the necessary wall access and drywall patching as part of the project. Paint matching is typically the homeowner's responsibility unless discussed otherwise upfront.

Do I need to repipe if I've only had one leak?

Not necessarily. A single, isolated leak can often be repaired on its own. Repiping becomes the more sensible option once leaks are recurring or water quality issues show up at multiple fixtures.

Is repiping work up to code?

Yes. All repiping work follows California Plumbing Code standards for material, sizing, and permit requirements.
